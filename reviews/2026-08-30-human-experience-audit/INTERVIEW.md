## Codex

There is a standard and intuitive metric: the **effective number of participants**, calculated as the inverse Herfindahl index:

\[
N_{\text{effective}}=\frac{1}{\sum_i s_i^2}
\]

where \(s_i\) is each person’s share of total wealth.

It reads naturally:

- 100 equally wealthy people → 100 effective participants
- 4 equal holders → 4
- one owner → 1

It is the same concentration family used in economics as the Herfindahl index and elsewhere as the inverse participation ratio. [UNCTAD explanation](https://unctad.org/system/files/official-document/iteiia20072_en.pdf)

It does not literally count everyone with a positive balance. That would be useless under redistribution because everyone receives something. It measures how many equally weighted participants the current distribution is equivalent to.

My recommendation:
