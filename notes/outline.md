# Outline

The working narrative for **Merit or Math?** This is the human-experience source of
truth after the 2026-08-30 author interview. It fixes the argument and order of ideas;
exact jokes, timings, and final sentences still require authored prototypes.

Only evidence-cleared model claims may enter published prose. The equal-agent model is
the experiment, not an apology for missing realism.

## Narrative contract

- Hamed narrates in the first person: playful, concrete, provocative, and never an
  anonymous explainer.
- The reader encounters a question, commits, acts, watches the result, and only then
  receives interpretation.
- Reader-facing runs use fresh randomness. Reversing or revisiting a completed run
  replays its stored result; only an explicit new run draws again.
- The model removes talent, effort, intelligence, inheritance, genetics, privilege,
  power, and every other personal difference. It asks whether those differences are
  necessary for concentration. They are not.
- “Fair” has one narrow meaning: random partners, the same stake rule, and an
  independent fifty-fifty coin. It does not mean equal exposure or moral justice.
- The demonstrated intervention is a periodic wealth levy **plus equal social return**.
  Collection alone is not the mechanism.
- Ordinary readers learn effective participants and ordinary wealth turnover. Gini is
  secondary technical material and remains available in the sandbox.
- No prose calls the stake-versus-levy outcome map a phase transition, a frontier
  between regimes, or a statement about how the world ends.
- Everything instructional happens before the sandbox. After it come only methods,
  references, credits, links, and clearly separated support material.

## The five acts

### Act I — The question

**1. The historical headline**

A real Reuters report, visibly dated June 14, 2026, says the world has its first
trillionaire. The estimate is on paper and moves with asset prices. Musk is a familiar
doorway, not a defendant the toy model claims to explain.

The opening also makes the author visible: created and directed by Hamed, linked to his
public GitHub profile. The cinematic opening remains skippable.

**2. Merit, or math?**

The title reel cycles familiar explanations and settles on “Math.” It constructs the
question; it does not announce the final verdict.

**3. The spherical cow**

Keep the farmer, three scientists, the physicist’s delay, and the absurd answer. The
silences are one timed comic sequence rather than several compulsory gestures.

The lesson is model literacy: remove details until a mechanism can be seen alone. The
minimal model can establish that a mechanism is sufficient. It cannot establish that
every omitted real-world mechanism is powerless. The current football conclusion is
discarded because it overstates that inference.

### Act II — The fair game

**4. The spherical human**

Reduce a recognizable person to one variable: wealth, drawn as circle area. “You are
your money and nothing else” is intentionally brutal and defines the model boundary; it
is not a claim about real people.

**5. Three teaching trades**

Retain all three rounds:

1. equal wealth and a fair toss;
2. unequal wealth and a stake limited by the poorer participant;
3. an extra return toward equality that strengthens the intuitive expectation.

Expose the distinction the second round creates: same coin, same absolute amount,
different proportional risk. Wealth does not load the coin; it provides staying power.

**6. Prediction and main run**

The prediction remains optional. The authentic run begins only when the reader starts
it. Result prose is generated from the actual top share and winner. A repeated winner is
a legitimate event, not an error. A deliberate rerun receives fresh randomness.

**7. The winner’s newspaper**

Generate a confident success story from traits assigned before the run but never used
by pairing, stake, or coin. Recall the opening headline. Show the habit of inventing
winner mythology after the result; do not name a cognitive bias in the main narration.

### Act III — Read the room

**8. Histogram and multiplying ruler**

Move the participants the reader watched into wealth bins. Teach that binning is a
choice. Then introduce the log ruler only to reveal ratios hidden by the linear scale.
Do not infer a power law.

**9. Effective participants**

Introduce the inverse-Herfindahl effective count as “how many equal participants this
room is equivalent to.” Equal wealth among 100 people gives 100; one owner gives one.
This is the primary human measurement because it expresses how many people remain able
to take meaningful part.

**10. Ordinary wealth turnover**

Measure the sum of stakes transferred through ordinary trades divided by total wealth.
Describe it as how many roomfuls of wealth changed hands per round. Exclude every levy
and dividend transfer. Trades can continue while meaningful turnover collapses.

**11. Larger room, theorem, and stake**

Use the learned measurements on a larger fresh room. State the finite-agent convergence
theorem briefly, with its assumptions and asymptotic limitation. Put proof and sources
behind “Show the math.” Then let the reader vary the stake and observe how finite-time
speed changes without implying a different asymptotic destination for fixed positive
stakes.

### Act IV — Keep the game open

**12. Manual intervention game**

Keep the point-and-click game as play and relief. The reader targets holders manually
and the proceeds return equally. The goal is maintaining participation, not punishing
someone for being large. Increasing stake provides the difficulty staircase. Winning
is never a gate.

The manual targeted rule and later structural proportional rule are related but not
identical. The transition must say so.

**13. Teach the structural rule**

Use four trackable fortunes:

1. remove the same percentage from each;
2. show larger fortunes contributing larger pieces;
3. form one common pool;
4. divide it into equal-area returns;
5. reveal the net effect.

Then repeat the rule faster on a condensed room. This teaches both the mechanism and its
equalizing effect without a second redundant widget.

**14. Trade and return together**

Compare matched runs with the same fresh pairing-and-coin sequence, first without and
then with levy plus equal return. Report effective participants and ordinary turnover.
The conclusion is not that tax creates talent; it preserves the field long enough for
talent, effort, judgment, and risk-taking to matter.

**15. The measured outcome map**

Map stake against periodic levy using standardized independent runs. Color continuously
by effective participants. A selected participation contour is a challenge target, not
a critical phase boundary. Reader runs remain noisy and pulse into the aggregate rather
than becoming a permanent second layer.

Use the map to discover the measured relationship `tax ≈ c × stake²` over its calibrated
range. The natural comparison is redistribution intensity relative to transaction
variance. The important interpretation is that a levy numerically much smaller than an
individual stake can preserve broad participation.

### Act V — The laboratory

**16. Earned conclusion**

Before handing over control, establish four points without preaching:

1. the coin remained fair;
2. money did not improve the odds; it kept its owner at the table;
3. condensation closed meaningful participation;
4. a small shared counterforce kept the field open.

The final authored wording and slogan require a dedicated Hamed voice pass.

**17. Sandbox**

Begin a fresh room. Preserve standardized outcome-map evidence gathered before it.
Expose the serious laboratory: effective participants by default, Gini as a technical
alternative, ordinary turnover, plots, population, stake, levy rate, levy frequency,
and deliberately raw expert inputs. The reader now has full control and no more guided
argument follows.

## Evidence and reader gates

- Every generated interpretation must match its observed run.
- Ensemble claims use independent seeds and a recorded protocol.
- The theorem remains distinct from finite simulation evidence.
- Formative studies test area reading, histogram/log comprehension, retrospective winner
  attribution, effective-participant comprehension, levy-plus-return comprehension, and
  navigation timing.
