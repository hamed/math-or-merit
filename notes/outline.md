# Outline

The working reader journey for **Merit or Math?** This is a modular narrative order, not
a frozen screenplay. Sections may move as the prose and widgets develop; their concepts
and evidence requirements should remain intact.

Only evidence-cleared model claims may enter published prose. Open mechanisms and
research-dependent claims remain in `ideas.md` and `research/` even when this outline
reserves a future place for them.

## Narrative principles

- Write for an ordinary reader and teach each statistical tool immediately before it is
  needed. Histogram, logarithmic scale, and Gini each get one brief visual lesson.
- Use Elon Musk's June 2026 trillionaire headline as the topical hook, not as evidence
  for what caused his wealth or as a stand-in for the model's agents.
- Let the reader experience the fair-process paradox before introducing policy levers or
  extensions.
- Keep story animations event-driven and reversible where practical. Narrative order
  must not depend on word counts or fixed animation delays.
- Show repeated seeded worlds. One vivid trajectory is an example, not statistical
  proof.
- Name the model's narrow claim: fair local exchanges can produce concentration. The
  model does not establish that real inequality is inevitable or that merit is unreal.

## Chapter I - From person to model

1. **The trillionaire headline** - open with a dated, citable report that Musk became the
   first person whose estimated net worth crossed one trillion dollars, on paper.
2. **Merit, or math?** - reveal the title in two beats and pose the question without
   answering it. Briefly contrast familiar merit and hustle stories with the scale of
   wealth outcomes; any quoted social post needs its own source.
3. **The spherical cow** - use the joke to explain why scientists remove detail. The
   visual cow simplifies into a circle in a vacuum as the reader scrolls.
4. **Turn the abstraction on the headline** - morph the illustrated Musk into the same
   circle. The point is methodological: we are discarding biography to isolate one
   variable, not claiming biography is irrelevant in life.
5. **One variable: wealth** - reduce every model participant to a stable identity and a
   non-negative wealth value. Human traits may remain visible, but none may affect the
   baseline economy.
6. **Teach the visual encoding** - money entering or leaving a participant changes the
   circle's size. State explicitly whether wealth controls area or radius once that
   mapping has been tested and selected.

## Chapter II - The fair game

7. **One encounter** - choose two distinct participants uniformly, stake a fixed fraction
   of the poorer participant's wealth, and use a fair independent coin to transfer that
   amount. Explain the precise zero-expected-drift meaning of fairness.
8. **Walk through the rule** - use a few scripted outcomes and reversals to make the
   transfer legible. Label this as instruction, not simulation evidence.
9. **Ask for a prediction** - everyone starts equal; pairings and winning odds are
   symmetric. Let the reader commit to what they expect before the population run.
10. **Watch several worlds** - seeded runs show temporary reversals, different winners,
    and increasing concentration without presenting one trajectory as proof.
11. **Reveal the long run** - state the almost-sure finite-population condensation result
    for uniform pairing and fixed `0 < beta < 1`, with the asymptotic qualifier intact.
12. **Explain the winner after the fact** - all agents had arbitrary, causally inert
    traits before the run. Construct a conspicuous success story around the winner, then
    expose that the trait never entered the rule. Repeat with another seed and another
    winner. Frame this as a storytelling demonstration, not a psychology experiment.

## Chapter III - Learn to see the distribution

13. **From people to histogram** - move the agents from the last run into wealth bins so
    the chart grows out of objects the reader already understands. Choose binning only
    after testing it against cumulative alternatives.
14. **Why a log scale** - when the linear view compresses most agents near zero, show the
    same data on a logarithmic axis. Teach powers of ten and zero-counting with a short,
    concrete example before using the transformed view.
15. **Measure inequality with Gini** - introduce Gini only when the reader needs to
    compare whole distributions. Anchor `0` to equality and state the finite-population
    maximum `(N - 1) / N`; do not imply that Gini rises on every trade.
16. **Run the larger experiment** - combine the now-familiar agents, distribution view,
    and metrics in a larger seeded run. Do not label a tail as a power law without an
    appropriate fit and model-specific source.

## Chapter IV - Change the rules

17. **Change the stake** - let the reader vary `beta`. Separate the established
    asymptotic result for each fixed `0 < beta < 1` from the visibly different finite-time
    speeds.
18. **Redistribute by hand** - a direct levy mini-game makes collection and equal
    redistribution tangible. Disclose observation, timing, targeting, and budget limits;
    do not predetermine that manual action must fail.
19. **Apply a structural levy** - compare a periodic wealth levy and equal dividend with
    the manual interaction inside this toy process. Call it a wealth levy, not an income
    tax.
20. **Read the result** - use Gini, top share, and wealth floor to compare runs and seeds,
    not merely the most photogenic trajectory.
21. **Design a progressive rule** - if the evidence gate passes, let the reader shape
    marginal wealth-levy brackets or a continuous curve and test the rule on held-out
    seeds.
22. **Map parameter space** - a phase-style diagram is optional and research-gated. Show
    it only if a reproducible grid supports meaningful regimes or transitions; explored
    points alone do not establish a theoretical phase boundary.

## Ending

23. **What the toy leaves out** - production, income, debt, work, bargaining,
    institutions, behavior, avoidance, growth, and claims about actual people.
24. **Return to the headline** - distinguish a mechanism that can generate concentration
    from a causal account of Musk's fortune. Local symmetry does not guarantee global
    equality; that is the earned conclusion.

## Gated extensions

- Additive exchange contrast after the exponential-fit replication.
- Static topology after confirmatory local-rich and degree-scaling results.
- Subsistence, passive-return, monetary-policy, and wealth-dependent-access mechanisms
  only as separately named model changes with their own evidence.
- Physical-system analogies only at the strength supported in
  `research/physical-analogy.md`.
